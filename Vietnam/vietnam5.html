Saigon (Now Ho Chi Minh City), Vietnam Pictures

As I mentioned on my main Vietnam web page, during my first few months in Vietnam, I didn't venture off base much, since I knew I had many months ahead of me to check out Saigon. But after the Tet offensive in January, 1968, downtown was put off limits to us, so I never made it down there after that (bummer!), and hence, didn't get many pictures of that bustling city. Here are the very few I do have, all black and white, that I developed and printed myself in our on-base photo lab (hence the not so great quality!)

Click on any picture to see it full size.

The few times I did go downtown, I usually visited the harbor where small boats and big ships from all over, as well as local water taxis, regularly floated in and out.
I've always hoped this little girl was just taking a nap on the street when I photographed her and that she wasn't homeless, living out there alone and hungry.

A rather common sight on the streets of Saigon.
I've always liked taking pictures of interesting doorways.

This statue extolling the bravery of the Vietnamese soldier stood in downtown Saigon for many years. While watching the nightly news sometime after the war ended in 1975, I remember seeing a report showing the victorious North Vietnamese soldiers and Viet Cong tearing it down.
