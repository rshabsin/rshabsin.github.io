Asakusa and the Sensō-ji
October 10, 2026

The area around the Sensō-ji (浅草寺), the oldest Buddhist temple in Japan, was filled with people, both tourists and the faithful who came to worship there. To get to the actual temple, we had to mingle with the crowd and walk down an alleyway lined on both sides with all sorts of shops. At the time, my right hand was still not feeling very good, and jostling among the crowd made me quite uncomfortable. My hopes for it getting better before the cruise tomorrow were fading fast. Anyhow, here are some pictures of what we came upon in Asakusa.

Click on any picture to see it full-size.


Rickshaws for rent were popular along the main street. Some of the women we saw were dressed in traditional Japanese-style clothing which, if they didn't have their own, they could rent.


One of the alleyways heading towards the temple. There's another one off to the left.

Getting closer to the temple.




The Senso-ji Hozomon Gate (宝蔵門), the inner of the two large entrance gates that ultimately leads to the Sensō-ji temple. I'm still unable to smile.


After passing through the Hozomon Gate, the temple stood straight ahead of us.


When we got closer, Mei-O was elated to see the 觀音堂, the hall dedicated to her family's protector 觀音 (Guanyin). Inside, there were two small statues of her on either side of the main altar. Mei-O was very pleased to be here. It was something she was looking forward to.


Out in the courtyard, the crowd congregated to burn incense and view other areas of the temple grounds.


A small garden alongside the temple had a statue of 觀音 (Guanyin).


One final picture before we headed out of the teple grounds to catch the subway again to our next stop.


Leaving the temple grounds, the alleyway we took back to the main street was covered - and crowded.
